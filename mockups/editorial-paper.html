    The Future of Web Accessibility

    The article argues that accessibility is no longer a compliance checkbox but a fundamental design discipline — one that, when practised well, improves usability for every person who touches the web. It draws on recent W3C research and first-person accounts from disabled technologists to outline where the field is heading.

    Key themes from the article:

    • AI-powered interfaces will both create new barriers and unlock new possibilities — automated alt-text generation and real-time captioning are promising, but opaque AI decisions can be especially disorienting for screen-reader users who rely on predictable structure.
    • Design-system accountability is shifting upstream: teams that bake accessibility into shared component libraries catch issues before they ever reach production, dramatically reducing remediation cost and cognitive load for developers.
    • Legal and regulatory momentum (including the European Accessibility Act and ongoing ADA litigation in the US) is accelerating adoption, but advocates caution that compliance-driven approaches often stall at the minimum — genuine inclusion requires going further.

    The article closes with a call to treat accessibility as continuous practice rather than a project phase, and recommends the W3C Web Accessibility Initiative introduction as an entry point for teams starting this work.
